# Example: the opening and the first two rounds

A made-up salesperson, Dana, an account manager at a 30-person managed IT firm. All names are fake.

## Opening

> Before I start asking questions, tell me who you are: your name and title, your company's name, and its website. If you have a bio or notes about your work from another AI assistant, paste those too.

Dana: "Dana Ruiz, account manager at Northgate IT. northgate-it.example. No notes."

## Round 1: confirming the website

> I read northgate-it.example. Here's what I found.
>
> Q1. What you sell
> Monthly managed IT plans, one-time projects like office moves and migrations, and security add-ons. Is that the full list?
> My guess: Yes.
>
> Q2. Who you sell to
> Offices of 10 to 100 people, mostly professional services: law, accounting, medical. Right?
> My guess: Yes, with medical growing fastest.
>
> Q3. Where you work
> The site lists the Sacramento region. Do you sell outside it?
> My guess: No, you stay local because you do on-site work.
>
> Q4. What sets you apart
> The site leads with "one-hour response, every time." Is that what you lead with on calls too?
> My guess: Yes.

Dana: "Yes, yes, local only. On calls I actually lead with the flat monthly price, not response time."

## Round 2: the answers open new branches

> So far: three offers, small professional offices around Sacramento, and you lead with predictable pricing.
>
> Q1. Your role
> Do you sell new business only, or also manage existing accounts?
> My guess: Both, and the owner signs off on contracts over a set amount.
>
> Q2. Where deals live
> Where do you track open deals today?
> My guess: A spreadsheet plus your inbox, no CRM.
>
> Q3. Who signs
> At a 20-person law office, who signs a monthly plan?
> My guess: The office manager recommends it, and a managing partner signs.
>
> Q4. Projects as a door-opener
> After a one-time project, what's your move to turn it into a monthly plan?
> My guess: A short health report within a week, with what you'd fix first.
